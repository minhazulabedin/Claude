Three Voronoi Diagrams, Done Properly

Each cell is the set of points closest to one seed. Done correctly, every boundary is the perpendicular bisector of two seeds — a straight segment — and three cells meet at each vertex. Drag any seed to match the drawing; click empty space to add one; double-click a seed to remove it.

Why this is “proper”. The faculty's freehand cells had curved boundaries. For ordinary (Euclidean) distance the boundary between two seeds is exactly their perpendicular bisector, so real Voronoi edges are straight lines that meet three-at-a-time at vertices. This tool computes each cell by intersecting half-planes (one per other seed) — the exact Voronoi diagram — so what you see is geometrically correct.
