FROM BEING HEARD TO CREATING CHANGE
Five steps
anyone can take
What is lived experience?
What a person has been through themselves: living with a mental health condition, seeking care, recovering, supporting someone they love, or facing barriers to help.
Why being heard matters
People who have lived it know what actually helps. When their voices are heard and included, support gets better and stigma gets smaller.
“Nothing about us,
without us.”
People with lived experience belong in the decisions that affect them.
1
Speak
Don’t be afraid to share your ideas and concerns.
TRY THIS
Choose someone you trust and a quiet moment.
Start small: “I’ve been feeling…” or “I’ve noticed…”
You decide what to share, and how much.
2
Listen
Respect other people’s opinions and experience.
TRY THIS
Give your full attention and put the phone away.
Don’t interrupt, judge or rush to fix things.
Thank them for trusting you.
3
Understand
Hear from different perspectives and identify what needs to change.
TRY THIS
Ask: “What would really help you?”
Remember that one person’s story doesn’t speak for everyone.
Notice the barriers: stigma, silence, cost, distance.
Words matter when you listen
SAY
“I’m here for you.”
“Take your time.”
“Thank you for telling me.”
AVOID
“Just get over it.”
“Others have it worse.”
“It’s all in your head.”
4
Act
Work together and take small, meaningful steps.
TRY THIS
Check in on a friend this week.
Offer peer support, or go with someone to get help.
Include people in decisions that affect them.
5
Create change
Small actions can create a better community.
WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE
Less stigma, more understanding.
Better support that fits real lives.
Dignity, respect and belonging for everyone.
